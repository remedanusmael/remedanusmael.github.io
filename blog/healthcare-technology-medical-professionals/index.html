Why Healthcare Technology Needs People Who Understand Medicine

Remedan Usmael · 24 October 2026 · Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

Healthcare is full of problems that technology could help solve. But identifying the right problems, understanding their consequences, and building solutions that work in real clinical environments requires more than technical expertise alone.

A software engineer can build an impressive application. But does it address a genuine clinical need? Does it fit into the reality of an overcrowded hospital, a resource-limited healthcare system, or a medical student struggling to access quality learning resources?

These questions matter as much as the code itself.

I believe healthcare technology is strongest when the people building it understand the problems they are trying to solve. Medical professionals bring something invaluable to this process: firsthand exposure to the realities of healthcare, an understanding of clinical workflows, and the ability to recognize where better tools could make a meaningful difference.

This is not an argument against technologists. Their expertise is essential. It is an argument for bringing medical knowledge and technical innovation closer together.

For me, this belief became personal when I started medical school.

From the beginning, I developed an ambition to contribute to healthcare beyond the traditional boundaries of clinical practice. I wanted to understand how technology could improve medical education, make reliable clinical knowledge more accessible, and help address the challenges healthcare professionals face every day.

I did not want to wait until graduation to explore that possibility.

I started learning technology and programming alongside my medical studies. It became a personal side project driven by a larger mission: to develop the skills needed to build meaningful solutions at the intersection of medicine and technology.

The journey has required learning unfamiliar concepts, exploring tools, and gradually turning an abstract ambition into something more concrete. I am still learning, but the direction has become clearer.

That direction led me to found Medinovance.

Medinovance is my effort to turn this ambition into something tangible: an ecosystem bringing together medical education, clinical reference, and AI-powered tools, with an emphasis on intelligent, adaptable, locally relevant healthcare technology and a global vision.

The mission is ambitious, and the work is only beginning. But I believe the best time to start understanding a problem and working toward a solution is before you convince yourself that you are ready.

My experience so far has strengthened one conviction: the future of healthcare technology should not be shaped by technical capability alone, nor by medical knowledge in isolation. It should be shaped by collaboration between people who understand healthcare, people who understand technology, and people willing to learn both.

I am pursuing medicine because I want to understand healthcare from the inside. I am learning technology because I want to help build what healthcare needs next.

And through Medinovance, I hope to bring those two ambitions together.

Medinovance is the ecosystem I'm building at this intersection.
