Why I'm Building Medinovance

Remedan Usmael · 24 October 2026 · Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

I study medicine. I build software. I've been building since I was a kid, and at some point those two things stopped being separate interests and became one problem: the tools I needed as a medical learner didn't fit the reality I was learning in.

The gap.

In the wards and classrooms around me, medicine still runs on paper, memory, and improvisation. That's not a criticism of the people — it's what happens when the tools weren't built for you. Most medical software assumes things we can't assume: stable connectivity, up-to-date infrastructure, guidelines written for somewhere else. The guidelines that actually govern care here — national standard treatment guidelines, local drug availability — live in documents, not in the tools clinicians open. And for learners, exam preparation means pulling from scattered resources that don't talk to each other.

I got tired of waiting for tools that fit. So I'm building them.

What Medinovance is.

Medinovance is a medical knowledge ecosystem, built for emerging healthcare systems, with a global vision. I founded it in Addis Ababa in May 2026. It has three parts:

Academy — live now in limited beta. A medical education platform for learners and exam preparation, with 2,800+ questions and counting.

Care — in development. Clinical decision support that puts localized treatment guidelines, drug databases, and clinical topics in front of healthcare providers across specialties in low- and middle-income countries.

AI — in development. An intelligence layer across the ecosystem, customizable to the needs of specific countries and healthcare facilities.

Why local — and why global.

Locally relevant isn't a slogan. A treatment guideline that ignores which drugs are actually available, or a learning tool that ignores how exams here actually work, is a tool that doesn't work where it's needed most. But the problems aren't only Ethiopian — emerging systems everywhere share them. That's why the vision is global: build for the reality here, and it translates.

Building as a student.

I'm building this as a preclinical medical student, which means exams come first and building happens around them. I'm building solo for now, and looking for a co-founder who shares the vision. Everything here is built in the open — if it breaks, you'll see it break, and if it works, you'll see that too.

What's next.

Academy is live in limited beta today. Care is the next mountain. The goal is simple, and it hasn't changed since the first line of code: advance medical knowledge through technology that actually fits the places it's meant to serve.

If you're a learner, a clinician, or someone building health technology for emerging systems, I'd like to hear from you.
